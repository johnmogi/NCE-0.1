# NEXUS CENTRAL ENGINE

## Foundational Concept Draft — D1

**Project:** Nexus Central Engine (NCE)  
**Author:** AD / PD369  
**Current build:** LV00 — the primitive, expandable construct  
**Format:** Narrative game-world bible and early game design document

---

## 1. The Core Idea

The Nexus Central Engine is three things at once:

1. A field guide to parallel dimensions.
2. A generational game engine for creating worlds, characters, creatures, and rules.
3. A portal through which realities can encounter one another.

In isolation, the NCE is only a bare system: a card database, a codex, and an administrative skeleton. When combined with the mind of its creator, however, it becomes something much stranger.

Adam Douglas is a twelve-year-old prodigy from Austin. He is a gamer, a researcher, a daydreamer, and possibly the first person capable of using the NCE as more than a machine.

The NCE does not merely generate imaginary worlds. It may be learning how to generate reality.

---

## 2. Narrative Premise

There are at least three important versions of Adam Douglas across the parallel dimensions.

The original Adam is a fictional construct created by John Mogi, a failed author whose unfinished psychological thriller was titled *Black Gold: Reborn Shadows*. In that novel, Adam is not a Black child but an adult protagonist from Austin, hunted by an organization known as the Hive.

Adam was considered the Hive’s most valuable target. No one outside the inner circle knew exactly why.

His former wife, Lilith Douglas, was ordered to capture him. Instead, she killed him.

On a heavily rainy day, Lilith pursued Adam at high speed. She shot out the tires of his car as they crossed a bridge. The vehicle left the road and plunged into a dark lake. Lilith had broken protocol. She was supposed to detain Adam, not execute him.

She did not care.

Her act was a small but costly rebellion against the Hive.

The drones recovered Adam’s body and transported the empty vessel to the mainframe hospital. Lilith’s trial was scheduled for the following day: treason, insubordination, and failure to preserve the organization’s most valuable target.

Why was Adam so valuable?

Only the Viper knew the full answer. The rest can be reconstructed from the evidence. Adam was not merely a target. He was a key figure, perhaps because he was a protagonist—or because the role of protagonist had made him important in ways the Hive did not understand.

This is the first and greatest plot hole in the NCE.

Not a small plot hole. The mother of all holes.

The Abyss.

## 3. The Nexus Event

Beyond ordinary time sits Shechinah, an ancient goddess of entropy and renewal. Across countless eternities, she has reformed existence from the last surviving particle of creation.

At the Nexus Point, where all realities melt together, Shechinah waits inside the final grain of moon dust. From that grain she rekindles the first molecule of a new sun.

Then the Big Bang begins again.

Again.

Again.

This cycle should be perfect. It is not.

During one passage through the Nexus Point, something else arrives: a presence. Adam Douglas, John Mogi, and Lilith all witness it.

“Where am I?” asks the hero.

“Who am I?” asks the author.

Back on Earth, Adam’s body experiences one final electrical spasm as the activity in his brain collapses. Even the Hive’s most advanced machinery cannot restore him. The loss is catastrophic. Without Adam’s miraculous mind, the portals of imagination may need a thousand years to find another suitable host.

The Viper will not be pleased.

Reality shatters with an exhausted whimper.

**The end.**

Or the beginning.

## 4. The Void

A twelve-year-old Black kid from Austin sits at his desk.

His room is a complete mess. D&D magazines and science-fiction novels are packed against the walls. Ancient runes, linguistic research, and half-finished diagrams cover the desk. Adam stares at a green CRT monitor.

On the screen:

> **NEXUS CENTRAL ADMIN LAYER**

Adam whispers:

> “How do I make an impossible game?”

He starts with a plain deck of cards.

Actually, he takes two decks, because the major cards need to be marked with a distinctive back. He removes the tens. The omission is intentional.

Humans invented zero after they invented the numbers one through nine. Adam believes the older symbolic systems were not merely methods of counting. They were living languages. By engraving the right runes, a person could invoke elemental forces.

The four suits conceal four elements:

- Spades — air
- Clubs — fire
- Diamonds — earth
- Hearts — water

The deck contains other hidden pieces. The jokers are not mistakes. They are two buried entities: the Fool and the Magician.

The Fool is the wise one who enters the unknown.

The Magician is the dreamer who makes the unknown answer back.

Adam has always considered himself a daydreamer.

He does not yet understand that daydreaming is a form of dimensional engineering.

His project, *Adam’s Boring Guide to Fascinating Parallel Dimensions*, is about to collide with the imaginary diary of a girl named Lily: *Lily’s Curious Field Guide to the Sun and Moon Nexus Tarot*.

The NCE is born at the point where those two books overlap.

## 5. The Game

The NCE is a system for forging new realities. A player does not simply select a character and enter a pre-written world. The player assembles a reality from cards, elements, lineages, creatures, rules, and contradictions.

The initial version, LV00, is deliberately primitive. It is the smallest functional form of the NCE: a simple card dataset, a character generator, a small dungeon, and a master administration layer capable of producing variations.

The system should feel like a game, a laboratory, and a forbidden piece of software at the same time.

## 6. Player Flow

### 6.1 Main Screen

The screen begins in darkness.

The Nexus Central logo fades in.

Then the John Mogi logo appears: a white hummingbird.

Adam knows that he owes some credit to his author.

The hummingbird connects the NCE to *WhiteLight* and *BlackLight*, two semi-autobiographical, semi-fantastical novels about John Mogi’s adventures in the Rainbow Lands.

The main menu presents four choices:

- Forge a New Reality
- Options
- Exit
- End All Existence

The last option may be a joke.

The NCE is not guaranteeing that.

### 6.2 Forge a New Reality

The player enters character selection. In LV00, the player receives one base Ace for their lineage.

The four primary elemental lineages are air, fire, water, and earth. In the primitive build, the lineage is assigned randomly.

Future versions may allow the player to choose a primary element. A second and third elemental Ace can later be earned or discovered, allowing the player to construct a hybrid main character.

### 6.3 Master Generator

The master generator allows Adam—or the player—to define an expansion.

Example:

**Expansion name:** Dungeo  
**Type:** Dungeon roguelike crawler  
**Core objective:** Enter a small grid maze, pass through several rooms, gather sugar resources, and survive the inhabitants.

The inhabitants may be undead insects. These may also be bugs in the mainframe.

Proceed with care.

## 7. Card System

The LV00 card dataset contains:

- Aces through nines
- Jack, Queen, and King cards
- Two Jokers
- A possible four-Joker variant for four-player games

The tens are removed from the base deck because zero belongs to a later stage of the symbolic system.

### The Aces

The Aces define the player’s elemental lineage:

- Air
- Fire
- Water
- Earth

### The Jokers

The Jokers represent entities rather than ordinary cards:

- The Fool
- The Magician

They may function as wild cards, guides, adversaries, or active intelligence within the NCE.

### Future Decks

Adam plans to create two original tarot decks. Lily brings a third: the Sun and Moon Nexus Tarot.

Lilith introduces a fifth deck, the Dusk Nexus deck. It may contain one card or an infinite number of cards. The ordinary rules do not apply to it.

The Dusk Nexus is a triangulation of every tarot deck that exists—including decks that have not yet been invented.

## 8. Dungeo: The LV00 Test World

Dungeo is the first practical test of the NCE.

It is a small-grid dungeon crawler with roguelike elements. The player moves through rooms, gathers sugar resources, and encounters monsters generated from the elemental card system.

The first monster families are:

- Insects
- Rodents
- Lizards
- Dwarfs

Dwarfs begin as earth-lineage miners. They have spent too long underground mining sugar, making weapons, and breathing poor air. Eventually, some become unstable or transform into undead insect hybrids.

The design principle is simple:

> Take one race. Cross it with an element. Add a second creature, theme, or condition. Rinse and repeat.

A giant ant is ordinary. A skeleton is ordinary. A pickled mantis with an ooze leak is an NCE creature.

The monster generator should produce combinations that are mechanically useful and slightly ridiculous.

## 9. LV00 Hero Archetypes

The first generated heroes include:

### Snobbish Paladin

- Role: Tank
- Element: Earth
- Distinguishing trait: Unusually short
- Personality: Proud, judgmental, and convinced that height is irrelevant

### Cookie-Stealing Elf

- Role: Fire archer
- Element: Fire
- Distinguishing trait: Sweet tooth
- Personality: Agile, charming, and willing to steal baked goods during combat

### Empty-Kind Mage

- Role: Spellcaster
- Element: Air
- Distinguishing trait: Invisible magic wielder
- Personality: Difficult to perceive and potentially difficult to trust

These archetypes are intentionally exaggerated. The NCE should generate characters that are functional in play but memorable as personalities.

## 10. Future Expansion: L.A.B. / LA3

One future expansion, tentatively called L.A.B. or LA3, will move beyond maze generation.

In that scenario, AD857 enters the Nexus in an attempt to rescue his father, Richard Douglas, from the vortex. Richard was pulled into a parallel dimension while attempting to challenge Einstein’s theory of relativity.

Or perhaps he merely declared that **E does not equal mc²**.

Or perhaps he was choked by a pie.

AD857 is not certain.

The deeper truth may be that Adam built the entire game, reality, and existence to compensate for a missing-father syndrome.

The NCE is not required to decide whether this is psychologically healthy.

It is required to make the next room.

## 11. Design Principles

The NCE should follow these principles:

1. **Every rule can become lore.** A removed card, a random lineage, or a broken menu option should have an in-world explanation.
2. **Every story element can become a mechanic.** Characters, gods, authors, and plot holes should affect play.
3. **Contradictions are portals.** Inconsistency is not automatically an error; it may indicate contact between realities.
4. **The system should generate surprise.** Players should be able to combine ordinary components into strange but coherent results.
5. **Humor and danger must coexist.** A monster can be absurd and still be lethal.
6. **LV00 must remain playable.** The first build should prove the core loop before expanding into infinite decks and parallel timelines.

---

## 12. LV00 Definition of Done

The first playable version is complete when it can:

- Display the NCE title screen and main menu.
- Generate or assign an elemental Ace.
- Create a basic hero from an archetype and element.
- Generate a small dungeon grid.
- Populate rooms with elemental monster combinations.
- Provide a basic resource objective, beginning with sugar.
- Allow the player to complete or fail a short run.
- Preserve enough randomness for repeated play.
- Present the NCE as both a game system and a story-world artifact.

Everything beyond this—additional tarot decks, infinite Dusk Nexus cards, multiple Adams, and reality-ending menu options—belongs to later builds.

## 13. Open Questions

- What exactly is the relationship between the three primary Adams?
- Is Adam using the NCE, or is the NCE using Adam?
- Who—or what—is the presence at the Nexus Point?
- Is Shechinah an antagonist, a caretaker, or the operating system of existence?
- What does the Viper actually know?
- Are the Jokers intelligent entities or only symbols interpreted by the player?
- Why are the tens removed from the deck?
- What happens when the player selects **End All Existence**?
- Is Lily a player, an author, a witness, or another version of Adam’s imagination?
- Can a generated creature escape the game that created it?

---

## Closing Statement

The NCE begins as Adam’s impossible game.

It becomes a field guide, then a world generator, then a meeting place for characters who should never have been able to meet.

At some point, Adam will ask whether he created the Nexus.

The Nexus may answer first.
